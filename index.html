Privacy Policy
One Minute: Feel Better

Effective date: 7 October 2026
Developer: One Minute Apps

This policy explains what the Android app One Minute: Feel Better (package com.oneminute.feelbetter) does, and does not do, with information. It is written in plain language and is meant to be short and honest.

In short

  • You do not need an account, and you are never asked for your name, email address or phone number.
  • We collect no personal data and no location. There are no ads, no analytics and no third-party tracking in the app.
  • What you read, pick and save stays on your phone.
  • The only time the app uses the internet is if you choose to send optional feedback, and that is sent anonymously.

1. What One Minute is

One Minute is a free self-help app: short quotes, stories, calming techniques and small questions to read when something feels heavy. It is not a medical, therapy or crisis service (see section 10).

2. What we do not collect

The app does not ask for, collect or send to us:

  • an account, login, name, email address or phone number;
  • your location;
  • your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files;
  • device identifiers or an advertising ID.

The app contains no advertising, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no third-party tracking. We checked: it is built only from standard Android and Kotlin components.

3. What stays on your phone

To work, the app keeps some things on your phone, in its own private storage. They are never sent to us or to anyone else, and we cannot see them:

  • which moods you pick, and when (this is what the "this week" look-back is made from);
  • cards you choose to save;
  • your settings (sounds, vibration, reminder times) and whether you have seen the welcome film and the first-use guide;
  • a small waiting file, only if you wrote feedback while offline (see section 4).

This information is not included in Android's automatic backups. You can remove it at any time: "clear history" in Settings removes the moods you picked, tapping a saved card's round icon removes it from Saved, and uninstalling the app, or choosing "Clear storage" for it in your phone's settings, removes everything.

4. Feedback (optional, anonymous)

In More → Feedback you can answer five quick questions and, if you like, type a few words. Nothing is sent unless you tap "send feedback".

  • What is sent: only the answers you chose and any text you typed. No name, email, device ID, location or account is attached, and the form does not ask for them.
  • Where it goes: over a secure (HTTPS) connection to a Google Form that we own. The answers appear in a spreadsheet that only we can open.
  • What Google sees: like any request over the internet, yours reaches Google's servers, which can see the network (IP) address it came from. Google handles that under its own Privacy Policy (policies.google.com/privacy). It is not part of the answers we receive.
  • If you are offline: the answers wait in a small file on your phone and are sent the next time the app opens with a connection. The file is removed once they are sent.
  • Please do not type personal information (your name, phone number, health details or anything that identifies you) in the text box. We read what you write exactly as you wrote it.

Because feedback is anonymous, we cannot tell who sent a particular answer.

5. Notifications

The two optional daily notes (a thought in the morning, a gentle line at night) are off until you say yes. They are created on your phone from the app's own library and timed by Android's alarm system. Nothing is sent to or from any server for them. You can switch them off in Settings, or in your phone's notification settings, at any time.

6. Permissions the app uses

These are all of them:

PermissionWhat it is for
InternetOnly to send the optional, anonymous feedback described in section 4. Nothing else in the app goes online.
Post notificationsTo show the two optional daily notes, if you turn them on.
Receive boot completedTo set those two reminders again after your phone restarts. It reads nothing.

Two other things are worth knowing. When you tap the share button on a card, the app makes a picture of that card on your phone and hands it to the app you choose through Android's share sheet; we do not receive it, and the temporary copy is kept briefly in the app's private cache and cleaned up automatically. The helpline button only opens your phone's dialer with the number filled in; it does not place a call.

7. Sharing of data

We do not sell, rent or share your data, because we do not have any: the app collects no personal data. The only information that reaches us is anonymous feedback you choose to send, and it travels through Google Forms and Google Sheets, which we use to receive it. Google Play, where you download the app, has its own data practices, which we do not control.

8. Data security

Feedback travels over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. What the app keeps on your phone is held in its private storage, away from other apps. There are no accounts and no One Minute servers, so there is no database of users that could be breached. The feedback spreadsheet is protected by the security of the Google account that owns it. No method is perfectly secure, but we have kept the app's data to the minimum so there is very little to protect.

9. How long data is kept

Information on your phone stays until you remove it (section 3). Feedback waiting to be sent is deleted from your phone as soon as it is sent. Anonymous feedback we have received is kept in our spreadsheet for as long as it helps us improve the app, and we may delete it at any time.

10. Not a medical or crisis service

One Minute is a self-help tool. It does not give medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, and it is not a substitute for a doctor, therapist or counsellor. The helpline information inside the app (for example Tele-MANAS, 14416, in India) is for guidance only: we do not run those services, and we cannot see or respond to anyone using the app. If you are in danger, or thinking about hurting yourself, please contact a trained listener or your local emergency number straight away.

11. Children

One Minute is intended for people aged 13 and over. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children, and the app does not track or profile anyone. A young person should use the app with a parent or guardian's knowledge, and should not type personal information into the feedback box. If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child has done so, write to us at the address below, tell us what was written, and we will delete it.

12. Your rights

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to look up, correct or hand over, and you are in control of everything the app keeps on your phone (section 3). If you are in India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives you rights over your personal data, including to ask for information, to have it corrected or erased, and to have a grievance heard. For any privacy question or grievance, write to us at the address below; we will reply as soon as we can. If you are not satisfied with our reply, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India as the Act provides. Anonymous feedback cannot be traced to a person, so we cannot look up one particular response ourselves; but if you email us the words you wrote, we will find that response and delete it.

13. Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, for example if a new feature uses information differently, we will update this page and the effective date at the top before the change reaches you. The newest version is always the one at this address.
